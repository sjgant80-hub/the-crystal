What it is, in one paragraph

Each lit facet is one step a small AI model got right, checked by exact arithmetic. A step it gets wrong is split into smaller steps. A step it still cannot do shatters, and whatever needed it stays dark. Nothing else is on the glass.

Start here

The words this page uses. A cell is one small sum the model is asked, such as 26 × 12. A reply bonds when code works the sum out in exact fractions and the reply is right; the bond-check is that code. A cell the model gets wrong is split into smaller cells; one that is wrong and cannot be split smaller shatters, and every step that needed it stays dark: it is left out, and that problem gets no answer rather than a wrong one. A round is one set of cells asked together, and a crystal completes when every cell from the problem down bonded. The Climb is the build before this one: it asked the model for a whole answer and checked it, and to bring a problem home is to get it right. The sealed run is the measurement, on problems the build was never tuned on, made after every file that could change a result was frozen. The watchers are code that attacks the bond-check: a harness forges replies, a second reader written apart must rule the same way on each, and a planted leak is a flaw put into a copy of the check on purpose, to show the harness finds it. A seed is the number that fixes every random choice, so anyone can make the same attack again. The depths 0 to 6 are layers of checking, from the step itself up to a person: nothing changes the model without a person's yes.

How it grows

A calculation is broken into cells. A cell is one operation on two numbers, such as 26 × 12. Every cell meets the same law, and the law is the whole engine.

  1. Seed. The crystal starts from steps that were already checked: the ones The Climb's sealed run verified. The seed on this page is 554 checked steps, from sjgant80-hub/the-climb data/runs/sealed.json at e27345f.
  2. Bond. The model is asked one cell. If its whole reply is one plain statement of that cell's value, exactly right (26 × 12 = 312, or 312 = 26 × 12, or 312), the cell bonds and a facet lights. A second line, a word, a unit or other working in the reply, and it does not bond. The value is the model's own; the check only says yes or no.
  3. Split. If the reply does not bond, the cell is replaced by smaller cells made from its own numbers: 26 × 12 becomes 26 × 10 + 26 × 2. Those are asked in the next round.
  4. Dissolve. If the reply does not bond and there is nothing smaller, the cell shatters. Whatever needed it stays dark, and the crystal does not complete.
  5. Depth. Every cell that is ready is asked in the same round. Rounds are the fourth axis: a cell's depth is the round it set in, drawn as distance from the centre and as colour, green-blue near the seed and violet at the deepest.

Two things follow from the law alone. A crystal that completes is right, because every step from the problem down to single cells bonded and a bond is exact arithmetic. And growth always stops, because a cell is asked once and each split is strictly smaller. In the published sealed run: 72 crystals completed and 0 of them were wrong; 0 ran into the limit on rounds; the widest round had 8 cells ready at once.

The watchers

The whole build is one stack of seven layers. Each layer watches the one beneath it and catches what that one cannot see in itself. The crystal on the glass is what these layers put out, and nothing else.

A stack of watchers only works if it ends, at both ends. The floor is depth 0: nothing is kept that does not come down to a step the model was asked and a check, which is code, bonded. The ceiling is depth 6: a person. Nothing on this page changes the model, and if a later build does, each change stops at a person first. Every layer between has a limit it cannot run past, written in its row.

What it cost

586 model calls for 84 crystals (7.0 each), 4743 tokens written and 73104 read, 1578 s of model time. The Climb's own loop on the same problems made 312 calls and wrote 14962 tokens. The widest round had 8 cells ready at once and the mean round 1.72: that is how many could be asked side by side on hardware that can. Here they were asked one after another.

What this does not prove
  • It is arithmetic. The finish line cannot be argued with, and that is why it was chosen. Nothing here measures the law on another kind of work.
  • The model did not get smarter. It is the same small model at the last cell as at the first. What changed is what it was asked: one small step at a time, each checked.
  • The checker holds the calculation. It knows what the problem asks, pairs the numbers, and makes the splits. The model does every step. For your own work, that bookkeeping is the part you would write.
  • Parallel is a property of the law, not of this machine. Cells of one round do not depend on each other and could be asked side by side. On one small GPU they are asked one after another. The page reports how many were ready at once; it does not claim a speed it did not measure.
  • Depth is not a fourth dimension of space. It is the count of rounds, drawn as distance and colour.
  • The attack on the bond-check is evidence, not a proof. It makes many forged replies, many ways, and a second reader written apart from the bond-check agrees with it on every one. That shows no leak was found by this attack. It does not show that no forged reply exists.
  • Depth 5, retraining the model, is not built. Nothing here trains the model on what the crystal proved. The wall on this page is where this model, as it is, stops.
  • A dark crystal is a result. Where a cell dissolved, the model could not do a step that has nothing smaller. Those problems are on the glass, unlit, and in the count.
How it is built

"Gated" means a mutation test changed that part's code many ways, one small change at a time (a < for a <=, a + for a -), and its tests caught every one. "Equivalent" is a change that makes no difference to what the code does, so no test can catch it; each has its reason written down in src/witness.baseline.json.

For your business

What it is. A way to get multi-step work out of a small model you run yourself, with a proof for every result it gives and a plain "could not" for every result it does not. In the published sealed run it completed 72 of 84 problems, none of them wrong, and left 12 dark.

Where it fits, and where it does not

It fits work that breaks into steps a program can check: reconciling figures, unit conversions through a chain, totals that must add up, any calculation with an audit trail. It does not fit judgement, open questions, or anything with no checker. If a step cannot be checked, it cannot bond.

For each role
  • Chief executive. The system cannot hand you a finished answer that is wrong: it finishes right or it says where it stopped. Both are counted on this page.
  • Finance. The cost is small model calls on hardware you own. 7.0 small calls per problem in the published sealed run. There is no charge per question.
  • Operations. Every result carries its steps. A dark crystal names the exact cell that failed, so a person picks up from there, not from the beginning.
  • Technology and security. One page, no server, no account, no key. The model and the checker run in the browser. The model's files come from the public WebLLM library, and this page does not check them against a fixed fingerprint (a hash), so a changed file upstream would not be caught here: a limit stated plainly.
Rolling it out
  1. Pick one job whose steps a program can check.
  2. Write the check for a single step. It must never be a model.
  3. Write how a failed step splits into smaller ones, and what the smallest steps are.
  4. Run your own cases and count: complete, dark, calls.
  5. Keep the dark ones. They show where a person or a larger model is still needed.
Security, data and ownership

A problem you run is handled in the tab; the page makes no request while it works, and the count is measured by the browser. Your runs are kept in this browser's storage so a long run can resume, and clearing site data removes them. MIT licence: run it, change it, ship it. Risks, answered straight
  • "It is slow." Each call is small, and there are many. 1578 s in all spent waiting on the model, for 84 problems on intel gen-12lp, asked one cell at a time.
  • "It only does sums." Yes, here. The way it grows (check every step, split what fails) works wherever code can check a step and break a failed one into smaller ones; this page shows it on sums.
  • "Some never finish." Yes, and they are shown. 12 of 84 in the published sealed run.

The case for the board. Most of the cost of AI in real work is the wrong answer nobody noticed. This is a small model, on hardware you own, that cannot give one without it showing. In the published sealed run: 72 of 84 finished, 0 finished wrong, 12 stopped and said where.

White paper: checked to the smallest step

The problem. The Climb showed that a small model gets further when code checks each line of its working. But a line was its smallest unit: a wrong line was worked again whole, and a problem the model could not hold in one reply stayed out of reach however many passes it was given.

The idea. Take the check down to the smallest unit there is: one small sum the model is asked at a time, a cell. A cell that bonds is exact and is never asked again. One that fails is split into strictly smaller cells. One that fails and cannot be split shatters, and whatever needed it gets no answer. So a problem that completes is right by construction: every cell under it was checked by exact arithmetic. Cells that do not depend on each other form rounds, and could be asked side by side.

How the check is trusted. The bond-check is the floor everything stands on, so it was attacked before anything was grown on it: forged replies of many kinds, a second reader written apart that must rule the same way, and twelve copies of the check with one leak planted in each, which the harness had to find. Each layer above it is a small module with a hard limit, and each is mutation-gated.

What this page tests. Whether growing by single checked cells completes more sealed problems than the same model answering once; whether, on the hardest problems that carry a calculation, it completes ones The Climb's loop did not; and whether any completed problem is wrong. The problems were sealed and the claims written down before the run; the numbers above show each one's result.

What it does not show. That it reaches every problem: a step the model cannot do even at the smallest size is a wall, and the page shows it in red. That the model got better: nothing here trains it; that would be depth 5, which is not built.

The one rule. A crystal that completes is right, because every cell under it bonded; one that does not complete gives no answer, never a wrong one.
